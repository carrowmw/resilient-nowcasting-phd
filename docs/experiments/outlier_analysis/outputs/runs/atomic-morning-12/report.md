## 1. Executive Summary
Across the 31-day window we flagged **8,022 outlier (node, timestep) pairs** (5.18% of observed steps, peak 75.0% of the network simultaneously), which merge into **1 distinct events** (1 network-wide).
- **Uncertainty is badly miscalibrated during outliers.** ZINB predictive variance does widen (×0.89 vs baseline) and zero-inflation π correctly falls (×1.00), but not nearly enough: 99% interval coverage collapses from 55.3% at baseline to **39.3%** during outliers. The model is systematically over-confident exactly when it should hedge.
- **Outlier information *does* reach zero-shot simulated nodes.** With outgoing edges masked (0 outgoing vs 1,810 incoming edges confirmed), simulated-node predictions still shift a median n/aσ during nearby real-node outliers (n/a of pairs shift >0.5σ) — propagation is real, but it rides on the *prediction* pathway more than on visible attention re-weighting (median attention shift n/a).
- **The kriging head degrades under neighbour-outlier stress** (RMSE 34.4 → 47.5, ×1.38), i.e. message passing propagates neighbour anomalies into held-out reconstructions rather than smoothing them out.
- **Scale-invariance holds.** At outlier steps the MSLE loss contribution of a high-traffic vs a low-traffic sensor differs by only ×14.32, versus ×3911 for raw squared error — TRUE MSLE is doing its job of equalising treatment across sensor scales.

Build-up dynamics, tripwire behaviour and the two-extremes proxy are detailed in §4 and the per-test sections below.

## 4. Recommendations
1. **Fix outlier calibration first (highest impact).** 99% coverage falls to 39.3% during outliers. Add an explicit calibration objective (e.g. quantile/CRPS or a coverage-penalty term) and consider learning a per-step dispersion temperature so θ can inflate faster. Outlier-aware augmentation (inject synthetic spikes during training) would expose the ZINB head to the tail it currently under-models.
2. **Add an event-detection / attention-temperature mechanism.** Predictions propagate to simulated nodes without attention concentrating on the source. A learned attention temperature (sharpened during high-residual periods) or a dedicated event-detection head would let the graph route anomaly information deliberately instead of implicitly.
3. **Make the kriging head outlier-robust.** Kriging degrades ×1.38 under neighbour outliers. Robust aggregation (attention with outlier down-weighting, or a Huberised kriging loss) should reduce the amplification of neighbour anomalies into held-out nodes.
4. **Fix the two-extremes export, then operationalise it.** The drop-NaN and fill-NaN branches currently produce identical numbers (gap ≡ 0 across all 1,612 real node-days), so the metric is presently useless. Repair the export in `export_paginated_payload.py` so the two branches genuinely differ, then expose the gap as a live per-node anomaly signal.
5. **Confirm real-world event attribution.** Cross-reference the top catalogue timestamps against St James' Park fixtures, concerts and road-closure feeds for the analysis window to convert statistical outliers into labelled events.

## Appendix — Method & Reproducibility
- Outlier rule: `|gt−μ| > 3.0σ` per node (σ = residual std-dev). Network-wide ≥ 30% of real nodes; spatial cluster radius 1.0 km.
- ZINB coverage uses `n=θ, p=θ/(θ+μ)` with zero-inflation `F(k)=π+(1−π)F_nb(k)`.
- Reproduce: `python run_analysis.py --run-id atomic-morning-12`. Raw results in `outputs/results.json`; figures in `outputs/figures/`.
- Training config (W&B): loss weights {'w_impute': 1, 'w_kriging': 2, 'w_forecast': 0}, seq_len 12, holdout frac 0.2, git `2be8aef047`.
